# Taeun Kang

Computational geohazard researcher working at the intersection of **landslides, debris flows, hydrology, numerical modeling, and high-performance scientific computing**.

My work focuses on developing physics-based and data-driven tools for geohazard forecasting, with particular interest in rainfall infiltration, slope stability, debris-flow dynamics, GPU acceleration, and AI surrogate modeling.

## Research Interests

- Landslide forecasting and rainfall-induced slope instability
- Debris-flow and sediment transport modeling
- Richards-equation-based infiltration and pore-pressure evolution
- Shallow-water equations and geophysical mass-flow simulation
- GPU / HPC acceleration for large-scale hazard modeling
- AI surrogate models for computational geoscience
- GIS-based geohazard prediction and environmental modeling
- Particle and continuum methods including SPH, MPM, VOF, and related numerical schemes

## Current Research & Development

### KIGAM-LF
Physics-based landslide forecasting framework coupling rainfall input, infiltration, pore-pressure evolution, and infinite-slope stability analysis for spatially distributed hazard prediction.

### KIGAM-DF
Debris-flow simulation framework based on depth-averaged flow equations with support for rheological resistance, erosion/deposition processes, and hazard-impact analysis.

### Scientific Computing & Numerical Modeling
I develop and evaluate numerical models using CPU and GPU architectures, with emphasis on computational efficiency, robustness, and large-scale environmental applications.

Current interests include:

- finite-volume methods
- shallow-water solvers
- HLL/HLLC-type Riemann solvers
- hydrostatic reconstruction
- adaptive and structured-grid computation
- CUDA/GPU acceleration
- surrogate modeling and model reduction

## Research Direction

A central question in my work is how to make physically meaningful geohazard models fast enough for operational forecasting.

I am particularly interested in combining:

**physics-based modeling + high-performance computing + machine learning**

to build scalable systems capable of predicting where landslides may initiate, how debris flows may propagate, and which areas are likely to be affected before or during extreme rainfall events.
